Start with what you notice

You do not need to understand the nervous system before using this workbook. A signal is simply a change you notice. It may show up in your body, thoughts, feelings, or actions. You are not trying to diagnose yourself.

01

Notice

Catch the first change you can notice in your body, thoughts, feelings, or actions.

02

Describe

Use plain words for how you feel. You do not have to know why it is happening yet.

03

Try one thing

Choose one small, safe step that fits how you feel in that moment.

04

Write it down

Note what happened before, what you tried, and what happened next.

Use it your way

Print: use the print button above, and write directly on the pages that matter today.

Reuse: print the one-minute pause, the daily log, or the new-step tracker several times, or slip them into sheet protectors and use a dry-erase marker.

Journal: copy the questions or table headings into your own notebook.

Safety + scope

Know when to stop and get help.

This workbook helps you notice patterns. It cannot tell you what caused a symptom. New, severe, or quickly worsening symptoms need medical care.

Get emergency help now

Chest pressure or pain · severe trouble breathing · fainting or loss of consciousness · face droop, arm weakness, or speech difficulty · sudden severe headache or new confusion · tongue or throat swelling · immediate risk of harm to yourself or someone else.

In the U.S., call 911 for immediate danger. Call or text 988 for suicidal or emotional crisis support.

Call your healthcare provider

Call when symptoms continue, feel unexpected, get worse, or begin after a medicine, supplement, cleanse, or treatment change.

The stop rule

Stop any exercise in this workbook if you feel worse, lightheaded, short of breath, numb, confused, or more upset. You'll see a short reminder of this rule on each practice page.

This workbook is for education and tracking. It does not diagnose, treat, or replace individualized medical or mental health care.

How your body shifts

Your body does not stay in one gear.

You do not need to feel calm all day. You need enough steadiness to think and choose.

Read the descriptions. Notice which one feels closest to you right now.

Revved up

Your body feels ready to act

Racing · tense · restless · jumpy · urgent

What may help: less stimulation, more support, a slower pace.

Steady + present

You have enough energy to respond

Present · connected · flexible · able to think

What may help: the goal is not perfection. It is having choices.

Drained

Your energy, focus, or connection drops

Heavy · flat · foggy · distant · hard to start

What may help: gentle movement, light, sound, or connection.

Mixed patterns are common.

You may feel tired and wired, frozen and racing, or numb and tense. Choose one gentle step, then notice what changes.

Start with the gentlest step that creates a helpful change.

When you feel revved up

Help your body feel more supported.

Choose one practice. Try it for about a minute. Notice how strong the feeling is before and after.

01

Look around

Keep your eyes open. Slowly look around and name five ordinary things you can see or hear.

02

Feel what supports you

Notice your feet on the floor, your body in the chair, or your hands against a steady surface.

03

Let your breath ease

Breathe in a way that feels comfortable. Let the out-breath get a little longer only if it feels natural. Don't hold your breath.

04

Make the moment simpler

Lower one source of noise or distraction. Do one task. Or try gentle walking, rocking, or stretching.

When you feel drained

Add gentle energy without forcing it.

Choose one practice. Start gently. Notice whether it helps without leaving you more tired later.

01

Look for light

Open the curtains or step into daylight if it is safe. Look into the distance, or name three clear shapes.

02

Change position

Sit more upright, press through your feet, or stand with support. Notice whether you feel clearer or more awake.

03

Add movement + sound

Try a short walk, gentle marching, tapping, humming, or a familiar song. Keep it brief at first.

04

Connect or do one thing

Send one message, speak to someone you trust, or finish one small task you can see.

Optional

If something new changes how you feel.

Timing helps you and your healthcare provider understand what changed.

Feeling worse is information.

It is not automatic proof that the plan is working.

01

Record it

Name the medicine, supplement, cleanse, food plan, or treatment. Include the amount, time, start or stop date, and what you felt first.

02

Check the day

What else changed? Meals, water, sleep, activity, illness, stress, bowel or urine changes, and other exposures.

03

Follow your plan

Use the stop, hold, adjust, or call instructions your provider gave you. Don't add several new fixes at once.

Get urgent help for tongue or throat swelling, severe breathing trouble, fainting, chest pressure, stroke signs, or immediate danger. Call your provider when a reaction continues, feels unexpected, or gets worse. Do not use this workbook to change medication on your own.
