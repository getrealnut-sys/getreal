A plain-language guide

What is integrative health?

Integrative health looks at the whole person, not one symptom at a time. It brings together conventional care and evidence-informed natural approaches like nutrition, botanicals, and lifestyle, so every part of your health is working in the same direction.

The short version

Your body isn't a set of separate parts. Integrative health treats it like the connected system it is.

Your blood sugar affects your energy. Your stress affects your digestion. Your sleep affects your weight. When those connections are ignored, people end up with a different answer for every symptom and no explanation for why they still don't feel well.

An integrative approach asks a different question: what is the body signaling, and what can we do about it?

How it's different

Two ways of looking at the same problem

Symptom-focused care Integrative health
The questionWhat treats this symptom?What's driving it?
The viewOne body system at a timeHow your systems affect each other
The timeShort visitsTime to hear your whole story

Both have their place. Conventional medicine is essential for diagnosis, emergencies, and many conditions. Integrative health fills in what a short visit can't.

Common misconceptions

What integrative health isn't

“It's anti-medicine.”

It isn't. It works alongside your doctor and your medical care, not instead of them.

“It's just supplements.”

Supplements are one tool. Nutrition, lifestyle, stress, and testing usually matter more.

“It's only for when something's wrong.”

Many people come to understand their body better and stay ahead of problems.
